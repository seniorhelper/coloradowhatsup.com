The Other Colorado: Art, Science, Fossils And Dark Skies

Everything worth doing in this state that does not require boots — which turns out to be most of it.

Edited by Zach Wennstedt · Updated October 10, 2026

ZW Zach WennstedtFounder & Editor-in-Chief · Raised in Boulder, Colorado

What is there to do in Colorado besides hiking?

A great deal. Colorado has an immersive art complex in Denver, a museum holding roughly 95% of one major American painter's life output, dinosaur tracks you can touch twenty minutes from downtown, an atmospheric research lab designed by I.M. Pei that is free and open to the public, and more certified Dark Sky places than anywhere else in the world.

Colorado's tourism image is a person in a puffy jacket on a summit. Fine. But the state also runs a serious cultural and scientific economy, and a rainy afternoon here is not a wasted day — it is an opportunity to see the parts of Colorado that the outdoor listicles have never once mentioned.

Denver, Which Is Not Just A Place You Fly Into

Meow Wolf Convergence Station is four floors of immersive art built by a collective of hundreds of artists, wrapped around a science fiction narrative you can either follow closely or completely ignore. It is genuinely unlike anything else, it is not a passive experience, and it takes most people three hours minimum. Buy tickets ahead.

The Clyfford Still Museum is the most unusual museum in the American West, and almost nobody outside the art world knows why. Still was one of the founding figures of Abstract Expressionism. He withdrew from the commercial art world and stipulated that his estate go, in its entirety, to any American city willing to build a permanent home for it. Denver did. The result is a single museum holding roughly ninety-five percent of one major artist's total life output — something that exists nowhere else for any painter of his stature.

The Denver Art Museum occupies two buildings that argue with each other. The 1971 Martin Building was designed by Italian architect Gio Ponti and is one of the first high-rise art museums ever built. The 2006 Hamilton Building is a Daniel Libeskind design of angular titanium planes that appears to be falling over. Inside, the Indigenous Arts of North America collection is among the most significant anywhere.

The Denver Museum of Nature & Science sits in City Park with a view of the entire Front Range from its west windows. Serious paleontology and anthropology collections, plus the Gates Planetarium. Bring kids or do not — it works either way.

Boulder's Federal Science, Which Is Open To You

Boulder holds one of the densest concentrations of federal atmospheric and physical science in the country, and a surprising amount of it is publicly accessible.

The NCAR Mesa Laboratory sits on a mesa above town in a building designed by I.M. Pei, who studied the cliff dwellings at Mesa Verde before drawing it. The concrete was mixed with local sandstone aggregate so the building would weather into the color of the Flatirons behind it. There is a free public exhibit floor on atmospheric science and climate, free parking, and trails leaving from the lot. It is one of the best free hours in Colorado.

Fiske Planetarium at the University of Colorado is among the largest planetariums in the United States and runs public shows and live star talks through the school year.

The physics done in Boulder's federal labs has produced multiple Nobel Prizes. If you have ever wondered why so many extremely specific atomic clocks live in one small Colorado town, that is the reason.

The Dinosaurs Are Closer Than You Think

Twenty minutes west of Denver, in Morrison, there is a road cut called Dinosaur Ridge where you can stand in front of the fossils rather than behind glass.

One side of the ridge exposes the Morrison Formation — Jurassic rock, roughly 150 million years old, containing bones of the animals everybody learned as a child. The formation is literally named after the town. On the other side, in younger Cretaceous rock, there are hundreds of dinosaur footprints preserved on a tilted slab, made when this was a coastal mudflat. You can walk the road for free or take a guided bus.

Further west, Dinosaur National Monument straddles the Colorado–Utah line and contains a quarry building constructed directly around a rock face holding roughly 1,500 fossil bones, left in place where they were found. You can put your hand on a 150-million-year-old femur.

Landscapes That Are Really Geology Lessons

Red Rocks Amphitheatre is a concert venue built between two 300-foot sandstone monoliths, and the acoustics are a geological accident rather than an engineering achievement. Free to walk during the day when nothing is scheduled, and the trail system around it is open year-round.

Garden of the Gods in Colorado Springs is free, always has been, and always will be — the land was donated to the city in 1909 on the explicit condition that it remain free to the public forever. Vertical red sandstone fins pushed upright by the same uplift that raised Pikes Peak.

Pikes Peak itself is reachable by cog railway, highway or a very long hike. In 1893 a schoolteacher named Katharine Lee Bates reached the summit, looked out, and wrote the words that became "America the Beautiful." The purple mountain majesties were these.

Great Sand Dunes National Park holds the tallest dunes in North America — roughly 750 feet — piled against the Sangre de Cristo range. Medano Creek runs seasonally at the base in a strange pulsing surge, though in a low-snow year like 2026 it may be thin or gone by early summer.

Mesa Verde protects roughly 5,000 archaeological sites, including some 600 cliff dwellings built by Ancestral Puebloan people. Cliff Palace requires a ranger-led ticketed tour and it is worth arranging in advance.

The Dark Sky Advantage

This is the category where Colorado genuinely leads the world, and almost nobody markets it.

Colorado is home to 13 International Dark Sky Parks and eight International Dark Sky Communities. More than 30 places statewide, including a dozen state parks, have formally begun the certification process — more than anywhere else on Earth. In late 2025 Breckenridge became the first mountain ski resort community in Colorado to earn Dark Sky Community status. Chimney Rock National Monument received a Dark Sky Park designation in December 2025, and Old Snowmass became the newest Dark Sky Community in February 2026.

New for 2026, the Colorado Tourism Office is launching a Colorado Stargazing Trail with itineraries and planning resources built specifically around night skies.

Practical advice for actually seeing anything:

  • Go near a new moon. A full moon washes out the Milky Way completely. This matters more than location.
  • Give your eyes 20 to 30 minutes. Dark adaptation is a real physiological process. One glance at a phone screen resets it.
  • Use red light only. Red preserves night vision. Most headlamps have a red mode nobody uses.
  • Dress far warmer than the daytime suggests. Standing still at 9,000 feet at midnight in September is a genuinely cold activity.
  • Check for smoke. In an active fire year, haze can ruin a clear forecast. The dry air that makes Colorado great for astronomy is the same dry air that makes it burn.

The Genuinely Odd Ones

Bishop Castle, near Rye in the Wet Mountains, is a full stone castle with towers, a dragon and iron catwalks, built almost entirely by one man — Jim Bishop — who started in 1969 and largely never stopped. There is no admission fee and there are no safety railings in the modern sense. It is a monument to stubbornness and it has to be seen.

The National Mining Hall of Fame and Museum in Leadville sits in the highest incorporated city in the country and tells the silver story properly, including the parts that ended badly.

The Royal Gorge Bridge near Cañon City hangs 956 feet above the Arkansas River and was for decades the highest suspension bridge in the world.

And Denver's RiNo district is essentially an outdoor mural gallery that repaints itself every year. Free, walkable, and different every time you go.

A Rainy Day Plan That Is Better Than The Sunny One

Here is the itinerary I give people when the forecast falls apart: Clyfford Still in the morning, walk across the plaza to the Denver Art Museum after lunch, drive twenty minutes to Dinosaur Ridge in the late afternoon when the light on the tracks is best, and finish at Red Rocks whether or not there is a show.

That is a full day, it is mostly indoors, it costs less than a lift ticket, and it covers Abstract Expressionism, Gio Ponti, the Jurassic and 300-foot sandstone monoliths.

Colorado is not only a place to be outside. It is a place that has been carefully collecting things for a hundred and fifty years, and a lot of them are indoors waiting for a bad weather day.
