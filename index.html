What this is

A Colorado Site Written
From Inside Colorado

Most "Colorado guides" are written by people who have never watched a summer thunderstorm build over the Continental Divide at one in the afternoon. They recycle the same twenty-five attractions, the same stock photo of Maroon Bells, and the same advice that was wrong three years ago.

This site is the other thing. It is six long pieces about how Colorado actually works — the ethics of camping here, what a century and a half of statehood built, how to read a mountain day before it reads you, what a record-low snow year does to a summer, where the climbing ladder starts, and the museums and observatories that never make the outdoor listicles.

Everything on this page is sourced. Where a number comes from the National Weather Service, the U.S. Geological Survey, the Colorado Department of Transportation, the National Park Service or History Colorado, it says so. Where something is a judgment call from twenty-odd years of doing it, it says that too.

The datebook

Colorado's 150th Year: The 2026 Datebook

What is happening in Colorado in 2026?

Colorado is marking 150 years of statehood alongside the nation's 250th — History Colorado calls the double anniversary the Sesquisemiquincentennial, and Colorado is the only state that gets one. Commemorative programming runs through December 2026, layered on top of the normal autumn calendar: aspen color, the elk rut, and the closing weeks of the Rocky Mountain National Park timed entry season.

The commemoration is genuinely statewide, not a Denver event. If you have been meaning to drive somewhere in Colorado you have never been, this is the year the state is actively rewarding it.

Colorado resources

Numbers And Sources
That Actually Matter

Five three-digit codes cover almost every situation you will run into in this state. Learn them once.

911

Emergency

Fire, medical, police. In the backcountry, know that cell coverage disappears fast — a satellite messenger is not paranoid here.

511

Roads

Statewide road conditions, closures and chain law from CDOT. Works from any cell phone or landline in Colorado, or use COtrip.org.

211

Community help

Housing, food, utility assistance and crisis referrals. Confidential and multilingual, covering all 64 Colorado counties.

811

Before you dig

Free utility locate through the Utility Notification Center of Colorado. Required before any excavation, including fence posts.

*277

Bad drivers

Dial *CSP from a cell phone to report aggressive or impaired driving to the Colorado State Patrol.

Note: 411 is directory assistance, not travel information. For Colorado road conditions the number is 511. Source: Colorado Public Utilities Commission dialing codes; Colorado Department of Transportation.

Common questions

Colorado, Answered Plainly

What is Colorado best known for?

Colorado is best known for the Rocky Mountains, its 58 peaks above 14,000 feet, and world-class skiing at resorts like Vail, Aspen, Breckenridge and Steamboat. It also has four national parks, 26 designated scenic byways, more than 300 days of sunshine in many areas, and the highest mean elevation of any U.S. state at roughly 6,800 feet.

How many 14ers are in Colorado?

There are 58 peaks above 14,000 feet in Colorado, according to the Colorado Geological Survey. The highest is Mount Elbert at 14,440 feet, which is the second-highest peak in the contiguous United States. Colorado also has roughly 647 peaks above 13,000 feet.

Why is Colorado called the Centennial State?

Colorado became the 38th state on August 1, 1876 — exactly 100 years after the signing of the Declaration of Independence. President Ulysses S. Grant signed the statehood proclamation, and the centennial timing gave the state its nickname. In 2026 Colorado marks 150 years of statehood in the same year the nation marks 250.

What is the best time of year to visit Colorado?

June through September is best for hiking, camping and scenic drives, though afternoon thunderstorms are near-daily from mid-July into early September. Mid-September through mid-October is best for aspen color, working from high elevation downward. December through March is ski season. May and early November are the quietest and cheapest, but many high mountain roads are still closed.

How long does it take to adjust to Colorado's altitude?

Most people adjust to Denver's 5,280 feet within 24 to 48 hours. Adjusting to mountain elevations above 9,000 feet takes longer — often two to three days. Sleep at a lower elevation for your first night, drink more water than usual, skip alcohol on arrival, and save your most demanding hike for day three. If you develop a headache with nausea that will not resolve, descend.

Do I need a reservation for Rocky Mountain National Park?

Yes, during the timed entry season. For 2026 the system runs from May 22 into mid-October, with two permit types: Timed Entry+ Bear Lake Road (5 a.m. to 6 p.m., ending October 18) and standard Timed Entry for the rest of the park (9 a.m. to 2 p.m.). Reservations are booked on Recreation.gov for a $2 processing fee. Additional slots are released at 7 p.m. Mountain Time the night before each arrival date.

Can I have a campfire in Colorado right now?

It depends entirely on current fire restrictions, which are set county by county and by each land management agency, and which change frequently. In dry years like 2026, expect Stage 1 or Stage 2 restrictions across much of the state. Always confirm with the county sheriff and the managing agency for the specific place you are camping, on the day you leave. When in doubt, cook on a liquid or gas stove.

When do the aspens change color in Colorado?

Colorado's aspens turn by elevation rather than by a single statewide date. High passes above 10,000 feet typically peak from mid to late September, mid-elevation areas between 8,000 and 9,500 feet peak in late September into early October, and Front Range foothills and city parks hold color into mid-October. Drought years push the whole sequence earlier and shorten it, which is the case in 2026.

What number do I call for Colorado road conditions?

Dial 511 from any cell phone or landline inside Colorado for statewide road conditions, closures and chain law information from the Colorado Department of Transportation. You can also use COtrip.org for a live map with traffic cameras. Note that 411 is directory assistance, not travel information.

Is Colorado in a drought in 2026?

Yes. Colorado ended the 2025–26 winter with statewide snowpack around 61% of median, the lowest reading in the modern snow telemetry record. Parts of Summit, Eagle, Pitkin, Lake and Park counties reached exceptional drought status, and much of northwest Colorado remained in extreme or severe drought. Expect fire restrictions, low river flows and an early aspen season.
