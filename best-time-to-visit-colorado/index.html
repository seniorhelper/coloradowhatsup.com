What is the best time of year to visit Colorado?

September is the best single month to visit Colorado. Crowds thin after Labor Day, afternoon thunderstorms fade as the monsoon breaks, the elk rut starts in Estes Park, and aspens turn gold from mid-month onward. June is the best month for long daylight and everything being open. February and March are the best months for skiing on a deep base. April, May and early November are cheapest, but many high mountain roads are still closed.

Colorado is not one destination with a high season. It is at least four different products that swap places on the calendar, and the worst month for one is often the best month for another.

Skiing peaks when hiking is impossible. The rivers run hardest in the weeks when the high country is still buried. September is glorious above treeline and unremarkable in Denver. Picking a month here is really picking which Colorado you want.

Below: all twelve months with real numbers, then the fall color question everybody actually came for.

The September question

Colorado Fall Colors,
By Elevation

When do the aspens change color in Colorado?

Colorado aspens turn by elevation, not by a single statewide date. High passes above 10,000 feet typically peak from mid to late September. Mid-elevation areas between 8,000 and 9,500 feet peak in late September into the first days of October. Front Range foothills and city parks hold color into mid-October. Drought years pull the whole sequence earlier and shorten it — which is the case in 2026.

The mechanism is worth understanding, because it explains why the advice changes year to year. Aspens do not turn because of a cold snap. The primary trigger is shortening daylight, which tells the tree to stop producing chlorophyll and reveals the carotenoid pigments underneath. Temperature and water stress modify the timing and the intensity, but they do not drive it.

That is why elevation matters more than the calendar. Higher elevations get colder nights sooner and are more water-stressed, so they turn first, and the color moves downhill at roughly 1,000 feet per four or five days.

2026 has a complication. Colorado finished the winter with statewide snowpack around 61% of median, the lowest reading in the modern snow telemetry record, and much of the Western Slope stayed in extreme or exceptional drought through spring. Drought-stressed aspens turn earlier, can look duller or browner, and drop leaves faster once they turn. We cover what that does to the rest of the summer in the 2026 water year.

The practical adjustment: shift your plans about a week earlier than a normal year, and favor north-facing slopes and drainages with reliable water. A grove above a creek will look dramatically better than one on a dry south-facing hillside a mile away.

Where to go, in order

  • Kebler Pass, West Elk Loop. Thirty miles of dirt road through what may be the largest contiguous aspen forest in North America. Peaks late September.
  • San Juan Skyway. The best overall fall drive in Colorado, and the reason the last two weeks of September are the hardest lodging dates of the year.
  • Maroon Bells. The photograph everyone has seen. Requires a shuttle or parking reservation.
  • Kenosha and Guanella passes. The Denver day trips. Both crowded on weekends; go Tuesday.
  • Lariat Loop. Holds color latest, into mid-October, and it is thirty minutes from downtown.

How locals actually count it

Colorado Has Five Seasons,
And Two Of Them Are Mud

Ask anyone who lives in a mountain town what season it is and you will get an answer that is not on the calendar.

Winter — late November to early April

Ski season. Resorts open on manmade snow in November and the natural base builds through midwinter. Mountain towns are full, lodging is at its most expensive, and I-70 becomes a weekly bottleneck. Down in Denver it is milder than visitors expect — the city gets bright sun between storms and snow rarely stays on the ground long.

Mud season one — April to mid-May

The resorts close, the snow melts, and the trails turn to soup. Mountain towns empty out, restaurants take their annual break, and some businesses close entirely for a few weeks. It is genuinely cheap and genuinely quiet. If you like a ski town without the ski town, this is the window.

Summer — late May to mid-July

Trail Ridge Road opens, the high passes open, rivers peak with runoff, and wildflowers come up in waves from the foothills to the tundra. Long days, stable mornings. This is Colorado at maximum.

Monsoon — mid-July to early September

Moisture pushes north from the Gulf of California and afternoon thunderstorms go from occasional to near-daily. Mornings stay clear. Afternoons build fast. It is still a great time to be here — you just have to be off the summit by noon. It is also the peak of wildfire and smoke season on the Western Slope.

Mud season two — mid-October to late November

Leaves down, snow not yet reliable, passes closing one by one. The second cheap window, and the last chance at empty trails before winter. Some of the best hiking weather of the year happens in the third week of October, on trails nobody is using.

The thing that surprises people

The Colorado Monsoon
Is Real And It Is Daily

When is monsoon season in Colorado?

Colorado's monsoon season generally runs from mid-July into early September. Moisture pushes north from the Gulf of California and the Pacific, and afternoon thunderstorms shift from occasional to near-daily. Storms typically build between noon and 3 p.m., produce lightning above treeline, and can cause flash flooding in narrow canyons and on wildfire burn scars.

People plan July and August trips because those are the warmest months, then get caught out by a weather pattern nobody warned them about. The important thing is that it is predictable. This is not random bad luck — it is a daily engine you can plan around.

Morning sun heats the ground. Warm air rises up the slopes. It hits cold air aloft, condenses, and builds vertically into a thunderhead. By early afternoon that cell is producing lightning. You can nearly set a watch by it.

Which produces the single most useful rule in Colorado: be off the summit by noon and below treeline by two. That is why fourteener trailheads are full of cars at four in the morning. It is not machismo, it is arithmetic.

Two other monsoon effects worth knowing:

  • Flash flooding on burn scars. Burned soil sheds water instead of absorbing it. An ordinary thunderstorm over a burn area can send a debris flow down a drainage that has never flooded in living memory. If a warning is issued for a burn scar above your campsite, leave immediately.
  • The monsoon is also what ends fire season. A strong monsoon is genuinely good news for the state. A weak one, in a drought year like this, means fire restrictions stay in place longer.

Winter

When Is Ski Season
In Colorado?

When is ski season in Colorado?

Colorado's ski season generally runs from late October or November through early April, with a few high-altitude resorts opening earliest and closing latest. February usually offers the deepest base, March brings the biggest storms and the spring break crowds, and the last weeks of the season deliver warm bluebird days on softening snow at a fraction of holiday prices.

The rhythm of a Colorado ski season, in the order it actually happens:

  • Late October to November. Opening day, usually on a narrow ribbon of manmade snow. Enthusiasts only.
  • Christmas to New Year. The most expensive and most crowded week of the year, by a wide margin. Book months out or avoid entirely.
  • January. Cold, quiet, and often excellent. The best value inside the core season.
  • February. Deepest base. Reliable conditions. The month to target if snow quality is the priority.
  • March. Colorado's snowiest month at many resorts, but also spring break. Big storms and big lift lines in the same week.
  • April. Softening snow, sunshine, patio weather and half-empty slopes. The locals' favorite and the tourists' blind spot.

Two practical notes. Buy a multi-resort season pass in spring for the following winter — the price roughly doubles by December. And plan around the I-70 rhythm: westbound Friday afternoon and eastbound Sunday afternoon are the worst blocks of the week. CDOT activates traction and chain restrictions during storms, and the fines escalate sharply if you block the road.

In a low-snow year like 2025–26, resorts lean harder on snowmaking and terrain opens later. Check the specific mountain's open-terrain report rather than the seasonal marketing.

Crowds and money

The Weeks To Avoid,
And The Weeks Nobody Wants

If your dates are flexible, this is the section that saves you the most money and the most standing in line.

The hardest dates of the year

  • Christmas through New Year. Mountain lodging at its annual peak. Nothing else comes close.
  • The last two weeks of September. Peak aspen color plus perfect weather plus school-free travelers. Harder to book a room in Ouray or Telluride than on the Fourth of July.
  • Presidents Day and spring break weeks in March. Lift lines and I-70 at their worst.
  • July 4th weekend. Every campground, trailhead and mountain town simultaneously.

The weeks nobody wants, which are quietly great

  • Mid-January. Post-holiday lull. Cold, empty, cheap, and often excellent snow.
  • Late April to mid-May. Mud season. Half-price mountain lodging. The Western Slope and desert canyons are at their best.
  • The week after Labor Day. Summer weather, summer access, and the crowds gone overnight. Arguably the single best week of the year.
  • Late October to mid-November. Leaves down, snow not started. Empty trails and surprisingly good hiking weather.

One more scheduling lever most people never use: go midweek. Colorado's crowding is overwhelmingly a weekend phenomenon driven by five million Front Range residents driving west on Saturday. A Tuesday in July at a popular trailhead can be quieter than a Saturday in October. The color is the same.

Common questions

Seasonal Questions,
Answered Plainly

What is the best time of year to visit Colorado?

September is the best single month to visit Colorado. Crowds thin after Labor Day, afternoon thunderstorms fade as the monsoon breaks, the elk rut begins in Estes Park, and aspens turn gold from mid-month onward. June is best for long daylight and full access, February is best for skiing, and April, May and early November are cheapest but with many high roads still closed.

When do the aspens change color in Colorado?

Colour moves downhill at roughly 1,000 feet every four or five days.

Is Colorado fall color early in 2026?

Yes, expect it about a week earlier than a normal year. Colorado finished the 2025–26 winter with statewide snowpack around 61% of median, the lowest in the modern snow telemetry record, and much of the Western Slope stayed in extreme or exceptional drought. Drought-stressed aspens turn earlier, can look duller, and drop leaves faster once they turn.

Storms typically build between noon and 3 p.m., produce lightning above treeline, and can cause flash flooding in narrow canyons and on wildfire burn scars.

When is ski season in Colorado?

Colorado's ski season generally runs from late October or November through early April. February usually offers the deepest base, March brings the biggest storms along with spring break crowds, and April delivers warm bluebird days on softening snow at a fraction of holiday prices. Buy a multi-resort season pass in spring for the following winter — the price roughly doubles by December.

What is the cheapest time to visit Colorado?

Late April through mid-May, and again from mid-October through November. Locals call these mud seasons: the resorts have closed but the summer trails are not ready, or the leaves are down but the snow has not started. Mountain lodging drops sharply, though many restaurants close for a few weeks and most high passes are gated.

When does Trail Ridge Road open?

Trail Ridge Road typically opens in late May and closes to through traffic in late October, with exact dates set by snow conditions each year. It is the highest continuous paved highway in North America at 12,183 feet. During Rocky Mountain National Park's timed entry season you need a reservation to drive it — the standard Timed Entry permit covers it.

What is the weather like in Colorado in summer?

Denver averages highs near 89°F in July and 87°F in August, with lows in the upper 50s. Subtract roughly 4°F per 1,000 feet of elevation gain, so a 9,000-foot mountain town runs about 15°F cooler. From mid-July into early September, expect clear mornings and near-daily afternoon thunderstorms that build between noon and 3 p.m.

How busy is Colorado in September?

Considerably quieter than July and August through the first two weeks, then very busy in the last two. The week immediately after Labor Day is one of the best of the year — summer access with the crowds gone. The final two weeks of September, when aspen color peaks, are among the hardest lodging dates in the state, particularly in Ouray, Telluride and Crested Butte.

What is mud season in Colorado?

Mud season is the local name for the two shoulder periods when mountain towns empty out: roughly April to mid-May after the resorts close and before the trails dry, and mid-October to late November after the leaves drop and before reliable snow. Lodging is at its cheapest, but many restaurants close for a few weeks and high passes are gated.
