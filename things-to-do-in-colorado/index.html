What are the best things to do in Colorado?

The four national parks — Rocky Mountain, Mesa Verde, Great Sand Dunes and Black Canyon of the Gunnison — are the anchors. Beyond them, Colorado's signature experiences are scenic driving on 26 designated byways, soaking in natural hot springs, hiking among 58 peaks above 14,000 feet, skiing from December through March, and stargazing in one of 13 International Dark Sky Parks. Denver adds immersive art, world-class museums and dinosaur fossils twenty minutes from downtown.

There is no shortage of Colorado lists. What there is a shortage of is lists that tell you the thing that actually ruins trips: half the best places now require a reservation you needed to book weeks ago.

So this page does two jobs. First, a filterable index of thirty-two places worth your time, sorted by where they are and when they are good. Second, a plain table of what currently requires a permit, what it costs, and when the window closes — verified for the 2026 season rather than copied from an older guide.

Colorado is bigger than people expect. Denver to Durango is roughly six and a half hours. Denver to the Great Sand Dunes is four. You cannot do the state in a long weekend, and the fastest way to have a bad trip here is to plan one that treats the map like it is the size of Vermont. Pick a region. Go deep.

Orientation

There Are Five Colorados,
Not One

What are the different regions of Colorado?

Colorado divides into five practical travel regions: the Front Range urban corridor along I-25, the High Country of the central Rockies, the Western Slope beyond the Continental Divide, the San Luis Valley in the south-central basin, and the Eastern Plains. They differ by hours of driving, thousands of feet of elevation and entire ecosystems. Choosing one is the single most useful planning decision you can make.

01 · I-25 corridor

Front Range

Denver, Boulder, Colorado Springs, Fort Collins. Where 80% of the population lives. Museums, food, breweries, red rock, and foothills trailheads thirty minutes from downtown.

02 · I-70 west

High Country

Summit County, Vail, Aspen, Leadville, Estes Park. The ski resorts, the fourteeners, Rocky Mountain National Park. Highest average elevation and the shortest summer.

03 · Beyond the Divide

Western Slope

Grand Junction, Durango, Telluride, Ouray, Mesa Verde. Desert canyon country meeting the San Juans. Warmer, drier, emptier, and the best fall color in the state.

04 · South-central basin

San Luis Valley

Alamosa, Great Sand Dunes, Crestone. A high desert basin ringed by 14,000-foot peaks. Strange, quiet, and one of the darkest night skies in the lower 48.

05 · East of I-25

Eastern Plains

La Junta, Sterling, Lamar. Almost nobody goes. Which is why the dinosaur tracks, the adobe forts and the sandstone hoodoos out here are still yours alone.

The mistake first-time visitors make is trying to touch three of these in five days. Denver to Durango is about six and a half hours of driving each way, and much of it is mountain highway where you cannot make up time. Grand Junction to the Great Sand Dunes is another four. Colorado is roughly the same size as the entire United Kingdom, and it has mountains in the middle of it.

A better plan: pick the region that matches your season. High Country in July and September. Western Slope in May, June and October. Front Range and Eastern Plains any time. San Luis Valley in late spring or fall, when the dunes are not 150 degrees on the surface.

Do you need reservations for Colorado attractions?

For several of the most popular ones, yes. Rocky Mountain National Park, the Maroon Bells, Hanging Lake and Mount Blue Sky all require advance reservations during their operating seasons, and all of them regularly sell out. Showing up without one is the most common way visitors get turned around at the gate.

Three rules that cover everything else

  • Book federal, not resellers. Recreation.gov and the official chamber or agency sites are the real portals. Third-party sites charging inflated prices for "permits" are reselling something they do not control.
  • The last two weeks of September are the hardest dates of the year. Peak aspen color plus good weather plus school-free retirees. Harder to book than July 4th.
  • Arrive before the window or after it. Most of these systems only restrict the middle of the day. Dawn and evening are frequently unrestricted, less crowded and better light.

No ticket required

Free Things To Do In Colorado

What can you do in Colorado for free?

Garden of the Gods, Red Rocks Amphitheatre outside of show hours, the NCAR Mesa Lab exhibit floor, Dinosaur Ridge's public road, RiNo's outdoor murals, Bishop Castle, Bent's Old Fort, Paint Mines Interpretive Park, every national forest trailhead, and stargazing anywhere in the San Luis Valley. Colorado's best-known free attraction, Garden of the Gods, was donated to Colorado Springs in 1909 on the condition that it remain free to the public forever.

The genuinely useful insight about free Colorado is that the state's public land is enormous. Eleven national forests cover a huge share of the mountains, and hiking on national forest land does not cost anything. The fees you run into are concentrated in a handful of famous places with parking problems.

If you skip those four or five bottlenecks, most of Colorado has no gate at all.

The free list worth planning around

  • Garden of the Gods, Colorado Springs. Vertical red sandstone fins, paved trails, no admission, ever.
  • Red Rocks, Morrison. Walk the amphitheatre and the trail system any day nothing is scheduled. People run the stairs at sunrise.
  • NCAR Mesa Lab, Boulder. I.M. Pei building, free exhibit floor on atmospheric science, free parking, trails from the lot.
  • Dinosaur Ridge, Morrison. Walking the road past the Jurassic bone bed and the Cretaceous track slab is free; the bus tour is not.
  • Bishop Castle, near Rye. One man, a stone castle, an iron dragon, since 1969. No admission.
  • Bent's Old Fort, La Junta. Reconstructed 1830s adobe trading post on the Santa Fe Trail.
  • Paint Mines, Calhan. Banded clay hoodoos on the plains that look nothing like Colorado and everything like Utah.
  • Any dark sky, anywhere. The Milky Way costs nothing. Go near a new moon.

Timing

What To Do In Colorado,
Season By Season

Colorado has four completely different tourism products depending on the month. Booking the wrong one is how people end up driving to a pass that is still under twelve feet of snow.

Spring — April to May

The awkward season, and the cheapest. High mountain passes are still closed, Trail Ridge Road has not opened, and the trails below 9,000 feet are mud. But the Western Slope is perfect — Colorado National Monument, Mesa Verde and the desert canyons are at their best before summer heat. Rafting season starts as runoff picks up. Hotel rates in mountain towns bottom out between ski season and summer.

In a drought year like 2026, spring runoff arrives earlier and drops off faster, so the big-water rafting window compresses. Ask the outfitter what the gauge is reading.

Summer — June to August

Everything is open and everyone is here. Trail Ridge Road, the high passes, the fourteeners, the alpine lakes. It is also the season with the most rules: this is when the reservation systems above are all running.

Two things to plan around. Afternoon thunderstorms build almost daily from mid-July into early September, so summit early and be below treeline by two. And July and August are peak wildfire and smoke season on the Western Slope, which can turn a clear forecast into a hazy one.

Fall — September to October

The best month in Colorado is September and it is not close. Stable weather, thinner crowds after Labor Day, the elk rut in Estes Park, and aspens.

Color runs by elevation, not by date. High passes above 10,000 feet peak mid to late September. Mid-elevation spots like Kenosha and Guanella pass late September into early October. The Front Range holds into mid-October. Drought pulls the whole sequence earlier and shortens it, which is happening this year.

Winter — November to March

Ski season, obviously — but the underrated winter Colorado is hot springs, Nordic skiing, snowshoeing and empty desert. Shelf Road near Cañon City has rock climbing in January. Colorado National Monument is quiet and gorgeous under snow. Mesa Verde stays open with far fewer people.

The constraint is driving. I-70 westbound on a Friday and eastbound on a Sunday during ski season is genuinely brutal, and CDOT activates traction and chain restrictions during storms. Check COtrip or dial 511 before you leave, not at the on-ramp.

Kids and bad weather

Things to do in Colorado with kids

The altitude hits children harder and faster than adults, and it usually shows up as crankiness and a bad night's sleep rather than anything dramatic. Plan low for the first day.

  • Denver Museum of Nature & Science. Dinosaurs, gems, a planetarium, and a view of the whole Front Range from the west windows.
  • Great Sand Dunes. Rent a sandboard in Alamosa. It is the single best kid activity in the state and it wears them out completely.
  • Durango & Silverton Narrow Gauge Railroad. A coal-fired steam train on an 1882 route through a river canyon. Long day, worth it.
  • Dinosaur Ridge. Real footprints they can stand next to, twenty minutes from Denver.
  • Elk rut in Estes Park. September and October, dawn and dusk, from inside the car. Free and unforgettable.
  • Royal Gorge Bridge. 956 feet above the Arkansas River. Terrifying in the good way.

What to do in Colorado when the weather falls apart

Afternoon storms and early snow are normal here. A washed-out day is not a lost day.

The itinerary I hand people: Clyfford Still Museum in the morning, walk across the plaza to the Denver Art Museum after lunch, drive twenty minutes out to Dinosaur Ridge when the light on the track slab is best, finish at Red Rocks whether or not there is a show. Mostly indoors, costs less than a lift ticket, and covers Abstract Expressionism, a Gio Ponti building, the Jurassic and 300-foot sandstone monoliths.

Outside Denver, the reliable bad-weather answer is hot springs. Glenwood, Steamboat's Strawberry Park, Ouray, Pagosa. Sitting in 104-degree mineral water while it snows on your head is arguably the best thing about winter in this state.

If you did not come here on vacation

In Denver For Work?
Here Is Your Free Afternoon.

What can you do in Denver with only a few hours?

From the Colorado Convention Center, the Denver Art Museum, Clyfford Still Museum and History Colorado Center are all a walk of roughly ten to fifteen minutes. With two hours, do one museum. With four, add Union Station and Larimer Square. With a full free afternoon, drive thirty minutes to Red Rocks Amphitheatre — it is free to walk and it is the best thing within an hour of downtown.

Denver runs a serious convention calendar, which means a large share of the people reading this page did not choose Colorado — a sales kickoff, a trade show or a client meeting did. That is still a real trip, and downtown Denver is unusually good for it because the cultural district is genuinely walkable from the convention hotels.

Two hours between sessions

Pick one museum and commit. The Clyfford Still is small enough to do properly in ninety minutes and it is the most unusual collection in the American West. The Denver Art Museum needs longer, but the Indigenous Arts of North America galleries alone justify a short visit.

Half a day

Union Station for coffee in the great hall, walk the 16th Street corridor, then Larimer Square. If you have a car or do not mind a short rideshare, RiNo is an outdoor mural district that repaints itself annually — free, walkable, different every year.

A full free day

Red Rocks in the morning, Dinosaur Ridge on the way back, and you are showered and back downtown before dinner. Or Meow Wolf Convergence Station if the weather is bad and you want the strangest three hours available in the state.

And if you are here running the meeting rather than attending it — the difference between a good week in Denver and an expensive one usually comes down to what happens in the room, not what happens after it. Structured sales training is the unglamorous version of preparation, and it is the one that changes the number at the end of the quarter.

The other two questions

Where To Eat,
Where To Sleep

Two things determine whether a Colorado trip felt good in hindsight, and neither of them is the hike.

Food

Denver's restaurant scene stopped being a punchline about fifteen years ago and has been quietly excellent since. The concentration is in RiNo, LoHi and the Highlands rather than downtown proper. Green chile is the regional dish that actually matters here, and the Colorado version is its own thing — pork-based, thinner than New Mexico's, served over everything.

In mountain towns, the honest advice is to eat lunch big and dinner early. Resort town kitchens close earlier than you expect, and the good places book out on weekends in both peak seasons. If you are working through Denver specifically, our sister guide at Best Restaurant Denver goes deeper on neighborhoods and reservations than this page can.

One practical note for restaurant owners reading this: a growing number of Colorado places have moved their menus to tap-and-scan digital hosting rather than reprinting laminated cards every season. Link Restaurants handles that end of it.

Sleeping

Three tiers, and they are wildly different prices.

  • Resort towns. Vail, Aspen, Breckenridge, Telluride. Expensive in both peak seasons, genuinely cheap in the April–May and October–November shoulder gaps.
  • Gateway towns. Estes Park, Salida, Buena Vista, Ouray, Durango, Alamosa. Better value, more character, usually within an hour of the thing you came for.
  • Camping. Dispersed camping on national forest land is free and legal on most Forest Service roads, generally capped at 14 days in a 28-day period. Developed campgrounds book through Recreation.gov and the good ones fill months out.

If camping is the plan, start with Colorado Camping Spots, which breaks the state down region by region, and read our Leave No Trace guide before you go — especially the section on killing a fire in a drought year.

Logistics

Getting Around,
And What It Really Costs You

Do you need a car in Colorado?

Outside downtown Denver and Boulder, yes. Colorado's attractions are spread across an area roughly the size of the United Kingdom with mountains in the middle, and public transit between regions is limited. Denver International Airport connects to downtown by rail, but reaching trailheads, byways and national parks realistically requires a vehicle.

Four things people underestimate:

  • Mountain driving is slower than the map says. Add 30% to any GPS estimate that crosses a pass. Construction, wildlife and slow trucks are constants.
  • I-70 has a rhythm. Westbound Friday afternoon and eastbound Sunday afternoon are the worst four-hour blocks of the week, year-round, and dramatically worse during ski season. Leave at odd hours.
  • Chain and traction law is real. CDOT activates restrictions during storms and the fines escalate sharply if you block the road. All-season tires with adequate tread usually satisfy the passenger vehicle traction law; check before the storm, not during.
  • Some of the best roads are dirt. Kebler Pass, the Alpine Loop, much of the Eastern Plains. Rental agreements sometimes prohibit unpaved roads. Read yours.

For live conditions, cameras and closures, use COtrip.org or dial 511 from any phone in Colorado. Note that 411 is directory assistance, not travel information — a surprisingly common mix-up.

Planning Questions,
Answered Plainly

What are the best things to do in Colorado?

The four national parks — Rocky Mountain, Mesa Verde, Great Sand Dunes and Black Canyon of the Gunnison — are the anchors. Beyond them, Colorado's signature experiences are scenic driving on 26 designated byways, soaking in natural hot springs, hiking among 58 peaks above 14,000 feet, skiing from December through March, and stargazing in one of 13 International Dark Sky Parks. Denver adds immersive art, world-class museums and dinosaur fossils twenty minutes from downtown.

Do you need reservations for Colorado attractions?

For several of the most popular ones, yes. Rocky Mountain National Park, the Maroon Bells, Hanging Lake and Mount Blue Sky all require advance reservations during their operating seasons and regularly sell out. Book through the official agency or chamber portals rather than third-party resellers. The last two weeks of September, when the aspens peak, are the hardest dates of the year to book.

What can you do in Colorado for free?

Garden of the Gods, Red Rocks Amphitheatre outside show hours, the NCAR Mesa Lab exhibit floor, Dinosaur Ridge's public road, Denver's RiNo murals, Bishop Castle, Bent's Old Fort, Paint Mines Interpretive Park, and hiking on any of Colorado's eleven national forests. Garden of the Gods was donated to Colorado Springs in 1909 on the condition it remain free to the public forever.

How many days do you need in Colorado?

Five to seven days for one region, done properly. Colorado is roughly the size of the United Kingdom with mountains in the middle — Denver to Durango is about six and a half hours of driving each way. Trying to combine the Front Range, the High Country and the Western Slope in under a week means spending the trip in the car.

Do you need a car in Colorado?

Outside downtown Denver and Boulder, yes. Denver International Airport connects to downtown by rail, but reaching trailheads, scenic byways and national parks realistically requires a vehicle. Add about 30% to any GPS estimate that crosses a mountain pass, and check whether your rental agreement prohibits unpaved roads — several of the best drives are dirt.

What is the best time of year to visit Colorado?

September is the best single month — stable weather, thinner crowds after Labor Day, the elk rut and peak aspen color. June through August opens everything but brings near-daily afternoon thunderstorms and the most reservation requirements. December through March is ski season. April, May and early November are cheapest but many high roads are still closed.

What can you do in Denver with only a few hours?

From the Colorado Convention Center, the Denver Art Museum, Clyfford Still Museum and History Colorado Center are all about a ten to fifteen minute walk. With two hours, do one museum. With four, add Union Station and Larimer Square. With a free afternoon, drive thirty minutes to Red Rocks Amphitheatre — it is free to walk and it is the best thing within an hour of downtown.

What are the best things to do in Colorado with kids?

The Denver Museum of Nature & Science, sandboarding at Great Sand Dunes, the Durango & Silverton steam railroad, the dinosaur footprints at Dinosaur Ridge, watching the elk rut in Estes Park in September and October, and the Royal Gorge Bridge. Plan a low-elevation first day — altitude affects children faster than adults and usually shows up as poor sleep and irritability.

Is Mount Blue Sky open in 2026?

Yes. The Mount Blue Sky Scenic Byway reopened over Memorial Day weekend 2026 after nearly two years of construction on the upper road. Timed entry reservations are required, and reservations and fees are now administered by Denver Mountain Parks rather than the U.S. Forest Service. It is the highest paved road in North America, climbing over 7,000 feet in 28 miles.

Is Hanging Lake open and do you need a permit?

Hanging Lake is open and permits are available through December 2026, following the Forest Service trail redesign. Permits cost $12 per person from May through October and $10 from November through April. The trail is 1.2 miles each way with about 1,200 feet of gain, and the trailhead at I-70 Exit 125 is accessible eastbound only — from Denver you pass it, take Exit 119 and double back.
