Things To Do
In Colorado

Thirty-three places worth the trip, sorted by the season they are at their best, with the festivals and wildlife moments happening around them and the bookings each one needs.

Edited by Zach Wennstedt · Updated October 10, 2026

What are the best things to do in Colorado right now?

That depends on the month more than anywhere else in the country. In spring, head for the desert canyons and the Great Sand Dunes; in summer, the high roads, alpine lakes and Rocky Mountain National Park; in fall, aspen drives and the elk rut; in winter, skiing, hot springs and Denver's museums. Each season also brings its own festivals, from Telluride Bluegrass in June to the Great American Beer Festival in October.

A Colorado trip goes right or wrong on timing. The same pass that is a wildflower drive in July is under snow in March, and the canyon that is perfect in April is an oven in August.

So this page is organized by the calendar. For each season you get the places that are at their peak, what is happening around them, and the timing details that catch people out. The places are the same ones Coloradans send visitors to: four national parks, the big scenic roads, the hot springs, the odd roadside wonders and Denver's best indoor days.

Colorado is also bigger than people expect. Denver to Durango is roughly six and a half hours, and Denver to the Great Sand Dunes is about four. Match one region to your season and go deep, rather than chasing every place on this list in one week. For the full event calendar, see Colorado events month by month.

April to May

Spring: Canyons, Dunes And The First Runoff

Spring is the cheapest and most uneven season. The high passes are closed, Trail Ridge Road is still snowbound, and mountain trails below about 9,000 feet turn to mud. Lodging in ski towns drops to its lowest rates of the year between ski season and summer. Meanwhile the lower, drier corners of the state are at their best.

Where to go

  • Colorado National Monument, Fruita and Grand Junction. Twenty-three miles of rim road over red canyons and freestanding rock towers, comfortable in spring before the desert heat sets in.
  • Mesa Verde National Park, near Cortez. Ranger-led cliff dwelling tours start in early May (the 2026 season opened May 4). The park protects roughly 5,000 archaeological sites, about 600 of them cliff dwellings, and Cliff Palace can only be entered with a ticket.
  • Great Sand Dunes, Mosca. Before the sand heats up, the tallest dunes in North America, about 750 feet, are ideal for climbing. In late May and June, Medano Creek can flow across their base.
  • Black Canyon of the Gunnison, Montrose. A gorge so deep and narrow that parts of the bottom see roughly half an hour of direct sun a day.
  • Picketwire Canyonlands, La Junta. North America's largest dinosaur tracksite sits on a remote canyon floor with no shade or water. Spring and fall are the only sensible seasons.
  • Bent's Old Fort, La Junta, a rebuilt 1830s adobe post on the Santa Fe Trail, and Paint Mines Interpretive Park, Calhan, a free field of striped clay hoodoos on the prairie.
  • Front Range red rock. Garden of the Gods in Colorado Springs, deeded free to the public in 1909, and Red Rocks near Morrison, whose concert season traditionally opens with the Easter Sunrise Service. Dinosaur Ridge next door has Jurassic bones on one side and Cretaceous footprints on the other.
  • Rafting the Arkansas River, Salida and Buena Vista. The season starts as snowmelt rises. In dry years like 2026, the big-water window arrives early and ends early, so ask the outfitter what the river gauge reads.

What is happening

Boulder International Film Festival moved to April in 2026, and Memorial Day weekend brings Mountainfilm in Telluride and the BOLDERBoulder 10K. Sandhill cranes pass through the San Luis Valley in March. See our film and arts and wildlife guides.

June to August

Summer: High Roads, Alpine Lakes And Festival Weekends

Everything opens and everyone arrives. It is the season of the longest days and the most rules, because this is when the reservation systems run. Two things shape every summer plan: afternoon thunderstorms that build almost daily from mid-July into early September, so summit early and be below treeline by two; and wildfire smoke, most common on the Western Slope in July and August.

Where to go

  • Rocky Mountain National Park, Estes Park and Grand Lake. Alpine tundra, 355 miles of trail and the headwaters of the Colorado River.
  • Trail Ridge Road, inside the park. The highest continuous paved road in any U.S. national park, with eleven miles above treeline. It is usually snowed shut from late October to late May.
  • Mount Blue Sky Scenic Byway, from Idaho Springs. North America's highest paved road gains more than 7,000 feet in 28 miles to about 14,130 feet, and mountain goats and bighorn sheep are routine sightings. It reopened for Memorial Day weekend 2026.
  • Maroon Bells, near Aspen. Two 14,000-foot peaks over Maroon Lake, among the most photographed mountains anywhere.
  • Hanging Lake, Glenwood Canyon. A turquoise lake perched on a cliff, fed by Bridal Veil Falls, about 1.2 miles up with roughly 1,200 feet of climbing.
  • Pikes Peak, Colorado Springs. 14,115 feet by cog railway, highway or a very long hike; Katharine Lee Bates wrote "America the Beautiful" after reaching the top in 1893.
  • Durango & Silverton Railroad, Durango. A coal-fired steam train on an 1882 narrow-gauge line through the Animas River canyon. Plan the whole day.
  • Bishop Castle, Rye. A stone castle with towers, iron catwalks and a dragon, built largely by one man, Jim Bishop, beginning in 1969. No admission.
  • Rafting, still the top summer water activity, and dark sky stargazing at its best when the summer Milky Way rises over the San Luis Valley.

What is happening

June brings the Telluride Bluegrass Festival, the Food & Wine Classic in Aspen and the Pikes Peak International Hill Climb. July has the Greeley Stampede, the Cherry Creek Arts Festival and the Aspen and Vail classical seasons. August means Palisade peaches, the Rocky Mountain Folks Festival, Telluride Jazz and the Colorado State Fair. Details are in the music, food and drink and rodeo and fair guides.

Three habits help with every one of them. Book only through Recreation.gov or the official agency and chamber sites, never resellers. Treat the last two weeks of September, when aspen color and good weather coincide, as the hardest dates of the year. And remember that most systems only cover the middle of the day: early mornings and evenings are often open, quieter and better lit.

September to October

Fall: Aspen Gold, Bugling Elk And Beer In Denver

September is Colorado's best month by a wide margin: settled weather, smaller crowds once school starts, the elk rut and the aspens. Color moves by elevation rather than by date. Passes above 10,000 feet peak in mid to late September, mid-elevation spots such as Kenosha and Guanella passes from late September into early October, and the Front Range holds on into mid-October. Drought years such as 2026 pull the whole sequence earlier and shorten it.

Where to go

  • Kebler Pass, Crested Butte. Thirty miles of dirt road through what may be the largest connected aspen forest in North America, usually fine for a regular car when dry.
  • San Juan Skyway, Durango, Silverton and Ouray. A 236-mile loop through the most dramatic mountains in the state, including the Million Dollar Highway, and the best fall color drive in Colorado.
  • Elk rut in Estes Park, September and October at dawn and dusk. Hundreds of bulls bugle in the meadows; watch from your car and keep your distance.
  • Maroon Bells at sunrise, when the aspens around Maroon Lake turn.
  • Royal Gorge Bridge, Cañon City, a suspension bridge 956 feet above the Arkansas River that held the world height record for decades.
  • The spring list works again as temperatures drop: Great Sand Dunes, Colorado National Monument, Black Canyon and Picketwire Canyonlands.

What is happening

Labor Day weekend is the Telluride Film Festival. September brings the Breckenridge Film Festival, the Meeker sheepdog trials and Palisade's Colorado Mountain Winefest. October has Estes Park's Elk Fest, the Great American Beer Festival in Denver and the Telluride Horror Show. Our home page keeps a dated what's on now list.

November to March

Winter: Ski Towns, Hot Springs And Museum Days

Skiing is the headline, but winter Colorado is also hot springs, snowshoe trails, quiet desert and Denver's museums. Shelf Road near Cañon City has rock climbing in January, Colorado National Monument is beautiful under snow, and Mesa Verde stays open with far fewer people. The catch is the drive: westbound I-70 on Friday and eastbound on Sunday in ski season are punishing, and CDOT turns on traction and chain rules during storms. Our traction law guide explains what your car needs.

Where to go

  • Skiing and snowboarding, Vail, Aspen, Breckenridge, Steamboat and the rest. Buy multi-resort passes the spring before, not in December, when prices are much higher.
  • Snowshoeing and Nordic skiing on national forest land statewide: rentals cost about what a ski-lodge lunch does, and you may have a whole drainage to yourself.
  • Glenwood Hot Springs, Glenwood Springs, one of the largest geothermal pools anywhere and right off I-70, and Strawberry Park Hot Springs near Steamboat, stone-terraced pools in a creek canyon whose winter access road often needs four-wheel drive or a shuttle.
  • Denver's indoor day. Meow Wolf Convergence Station, four floors of immersive art built around a science-fiction story (allow three hours and buy ahead). The Denver Art Museum, whose 1971 Gio Ponti tower and 2006 Daniel Libeskind addition argue with each other, with a major Indigenous Arts of North America collection. The Clyfford Still Museum, holding about 95% of one Abstract Expressionist's lifetime output. The Denver Museum of Nature & Science, with serious paleontology, the Gates Planetarium and a Front Range view from the west windows.
  • NCAR Mesa Laboratory, Boulder. I.M. Pei designed it after studying Mesa Verde; the exhibit floor, parking and trails are free.
  • Dark sky stargazing. Long nights help. Colorado has 13 International Dark Sky Parks and eight Dark Sky Communities, with more sites in the certification process than anywhere else. Go near a new moon and give your eyes 30 minutes.

What is happening

Ullr Fest and Denver's Parade of Lights in December, the National Western Stock Show and the Breckenridge snow sculpture championships in January, Sundance's first Boulder edition from January 21 to 31, 2027, Steamboat's Winter Carnival in February, and Leadville Ski Joring and Frozen Dead Guy Days in March. Kids and bad weather

Two Lists Every Trip
Eventually Needs

Things to do in Colorado with kids

The altitude hits children harder and faster than adults, and it usually shows up as crankiness and a bad night's sleep rather than anything dramatic. Plan low for the first day.

  • Denver Museum of Nature & Science. Dinosaurs, gems, a planetarium, and a view of the whole Front Range from the west windows.
  • Great Sand Dunes. Rent a sandboard in Alamosa. It is the single best kid activity in the state and it wears them out completely.
  • Durango & Silverton Narrow Gauge Railroad. A coal-fired steam train on an 1882 route through a river canyon. Long day, worth it.
  • Dinosaur Ridge. Real footprints they can stand next to, twenty minutes from Denver.
  • Elk rut in Estes Park. September and October, dawn and dusk, from inside the car. Free and unforgettable.
  • Royal Gorge Bridge. 956 feet above the Arkansas River. Terrifying in the good way.

What to do in Colorado when the weather falls apart

Afternoon storms and early snow are normal here. A washed-out day is not a lost day.

The itinerary I hand people: Clyfford Still Museum in the morning, walk across the plaza to the Denver Art Museum after lunch, drive twenty minutes out to Dinosaur Ridge when the light on the track slab is best, finish at Red Rocks whether or not there is a show. Mostly indoors, costs less than a lift ticket, and covers Abstract Expressionism, a Gio Ponti building, the Jurassic and 300-foot sandstone monoliths.

Outside Denver, the reliable bad-weather answer is hot springs. Glenwood, Steamboat's Strawberry Park, Ouray, Pagosa. Sitting in 104-degree mineral water while it snows on your head is arguably the best thing about winter in this state.

If you did not come here on vacation

In Denver For Work?
Here Is Your Free Afternoon.

What can you do in Denver with only a few hours?

From the Colorado Convention Center, the Denver Art Museum, Clyfford Still Museum and History Colorado Center are all a walk of roughly ten to fifteen minutes. With two hours, do one museum. With four, add Union Station and Larimer Square. With a full free afternoon, drive thirty minutes to Red Rocks Amphitheatre — it is free to walk and it is the best thing within an hour of downtown.

Denver runs a serious convention calendar, which means a large share of the people reading this page did not choose Colorado — a sales kickoff, a trade show or a client meeting did. That is still a real trip, and downtown Denver is unusually good for it because the cultural district is genuinely walkable from the convention hotels.

Two hours between sessions

Pick one museum and commit. The Clyfford Still is small enough to do properly in ninety minutes and it is the most unusual collection in the American West. The Denver Art Museum needs longer, but the Indigenous Arts of North America galleries alone justify a short visit.

Half a day

Union Station for coffee in the great hall, walk the 16th Street corridor, then Larimer Square. If you have a car or do not mind a short rideshare, RiNo is an outdoor mural district that repaints itself annually — free, walkable, different every year.

A full free day

Red Rocks in the morning, Dinosaur Ridge on the way back, and you are showered and back downtown before dinner. Or Meow Wolf Convergence Station if the weather is bad and you want the strangest three hours available in the state.

The other two questions

Where To Eat,
Where To Sleep

Two things determine whether a Colorado trip felt good in hindsight, and neither of them is the hike.

Food

Denver's restaurant scene stopped being a punchline about fifteen years ago and has been quietly excellent since. The concentration is in RiNo, LoHi and the Highlands rather than downtown proper. Green chile is the regional dish that actually matters here, and the Colorado version is its own thing — pork-based, thinner than New Mexico's, served over everything.

In mountain towns, the honest advice is to eat lunch big and dinner early. Resort town kitchens close earlier than you expect, and the good places book out on weekends in both peak seasons. If you are working through Denver specifically, our companion guide at Best Restaurant Denver goes deeper on neighborhoods and reservations than this page can.

Sleeping

Three tiers, and they are wildly different prices.

  • Resort towns. Vail, Aspen, Breckenridge, Telluride. Expensive in both peak seasons, genuinely cheap in the April–May and October–November shoulder gaps.
  • Gateway towns. Estes Park, Salida, Buena Vista, Ouray, Durango, Alamosa. Better value, more character, usually within an hour of the thing you came for.
  • Camping. The budget tier: primitive sites along most national forest roads cost nothing, usually with a two-week limit in any four-week stretch. Campgrounds with water and toilets are booked on Recreation.gov, and the popular ones are gone months ahead, especially around big festival weekends.

If camping is the plan, start with Colorado Camping Spots, which breaks the state down region by region, and read our Leave No Trace guide before you go — especially the section on killing a fire in a drought year.

Logistics

Getting Around,
And What It Really Costs You

Do you need a car in Colorado?

Outside downtown Denver and Boulder, yes. Colorado's attractions are spread across an area roughly the size of the United Kingdom with mountains in the middle, and public transit between regions is limited. Denver International Airport connects to downtown by rail, but reaching trailheads, byways and national parks realistically requires a vehicle.

Four things people underestimate:

  • Mountain driving is slower than the map says. Add 30% to any GPS estimate that crosses a pass. Construction, wildlife and slow trucks are constants.
  • I-70 has a rhythm. Westbound Friday afternoon and eastbound Sunday afternoon are the worst four-hour blocks of the week, year-round, and dramatically worse during ski season. Leave at odd hours.
  • Chain and traction law is real. CDOT activates restrictions during storms and the fines escalate sharply if you block the road. All-season tires with adequate tread usually satisfy the passenger vehicle traction law; check before the storm, not during.
  • Some of the best roads are dirt. Kebler Pass, the Alpine Loop, much of the Eastern Plains. Rental agreements sometimes prohibit unpaved roads. Read yours.

For live conditions, cameras and closures, use COtrip.org or dial 511 from any phone in Colorado. Note that 411 is directory assistance, not travel information — a surprisingly common mix-up.

Common questions

Planning Questions, Answered Plainly

What are the best things to do in Colorado?

The four national parks, Rocky Mountain, Mesa Verde, Great Sand Dunes and Black Canyon of the Gunnison, anchor any list. Add the scenic roads (Trail Ridge Road, Mount Blue Sky and the San Juan Skyway), hot springs at Glenwood and Steamboat, and Denver's museums. The best choice depends on the season: canyons in spring, high country in summer, aspens in fall and ski towns in winter.

Do you need reservations for Colorado attractions?

Some of the most popular ones require them in season: Rocky Mountain National Park, the Maroon Bells, Hanging Lake and Mount Blue Sky. All four sell out. Book through Recreation.gov or the official agency site, and check each year's rules because they change.

What is there to do in Colorado in each season?

Spring suits the desert canyons, Mesa Verde and the Great Sand Dunes. Summer opens Trail Ridge Road, Mount Blue Sky and the alpine lakes, and fills the festival calendar. Fall is for aspen drives and the elk rut. Winter is ski season, hot springs and Denver's museums.

Do you need a car in Colorado?

Outside downtown Denver and Boulder, yes. Denver International Airport connects to downtown by rail, but reaching trailheads, scenic byways and national parks realistically requires a vehicle.

What can you do in Denver with only a few hours?

From the Colorado Convention Center, the Denver Art Museum, Clyfford Still Museum and History Colorado Center are all about a ten to fifteen minute walk. With two hours, pick one museum; with four, add Union Station and Larimer Square; with a free afternoon, drive about thirty minutes to Red Rocks.

When is the best time of year to visit Colorado?

September is the strongest single month, with settled weather, smaller crowds after Labor Day, the elk rut and aspen color. June through August opens everything but brings near-daily afternoon storms and the most reservation systems. December through March is ski season.
