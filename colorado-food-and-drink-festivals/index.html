What food festivals is Colorado known for?

Colorado's best-known food and drink events are the Great American Beer Festival in Denver each fall, the Food & Wine Classic in Aspen each June, the Palisade Peach Festival in August and the Colorado Mountain Winefest in Palisade in September. Much of the rest of the calendar follows the Western Slope harvest, from cherries and peaches to sweet corn, apples and wine grapes.

Colorado's food calendar has two personalities. One is a beer culture large enough to host the country's best-known brewing competition. The other is a farm season on the Western Slope that most visitors never see.

The beer side is concentrated on the Front Range, where Denver, Boulder and Fort Collins have built one of the densest brewing scenes in the United States. The farm side is concentrated in the Grand Valley around Grand Junction and Palisade, and in the North Fork Valley near Paonia and Hotchkiss, where warm days, cool nights and irrigation from the Colorado and Gunnison rivers make fruit and wine grapes possible at more than 4,500 feet.

Beer

The Great American Beer Festival

The Great American Beer Festival is run by the Brewers Association, the national trade group for small and independent brewers, which is based in Boulder. It combines a public tasting festival with a judged competition whose medals are among the most widely recognized in American craft brewing.

For most of its history the festival filled the Colorado Convention Center in downtown Denver. In 2026 it moved outdoors for the first time, to Levitt Pavilion in Ruby Hill Park in south Denver, with afternoon sessions on Saturday, October 10 and Sunday, October 11 and the competition awards on October 11. Format and venue may continue to evolve, so check greatamericanbeerfestival.com before you plan a trip.

Practical notes: the festival is 21 and over, tickets are sold by session, and Denver's light rail and bus network are easier than parking near the venue. Pace yourself; the standard pours are small, but altitude amplifies alcohol for visitors who have just arrived.

Outside festival week, the easiest way to sample Colorado beer is simply to visit a few of the state's breweries in person. Fort Collins, Boulder and Denver's RiNo neighborhood each have enough within walking or biking distance to fill a day.

June in Aspen

Food & Wine Classic In Aspen

The Food & Wine Classic is a three-day event produced by Food & Wine magazine in Aspen each June, centered on tasting pavilions in Wagner Park plus cooking demonstrations and seminars with well-known chefs and wine experts. The 2026 Classic, its 43rd, ran June 19 to 21.

This is a premium, ticketed event with limited capacity, and passes are expensive and sell out. If you are not attending, the weekend still affects you: Aspen lodging is at its most expensive, restaurants are booked, and parking is difficult. Visitors who want a quieter Aspen in June should look at the weeks on either side.

July to October on the Western Slope

The Harvest Festivals

Palisade Peach Festival

Palisade's peaches are the best-known crop in the state, and the Palisade Peach Festival celebrates the harvest at Riverbend Park. The 2026 festival ran Friday and Saturday, August 21 and 22, with a peach-eating contest, a paddleboard race and a Friday night drone show. Parking at the park itself is limited to accessible spaces, so most visitors ride the festival shuttles from Grand Junction or Palisade.

Peach season in the Grand Valley usually runs from midsummer into September. Many orchards sell from roadside stands along the Fruit and Wine Byway, a loop that winds through the orchards and vineyards east of Grand Junction.

Olathe Sweet Corn

Olathe, between Montrose and Delta, is famous for its sweet corn, and the town has a long tradition of a summer sweet corn festival. Recent listings for the event have been inconsistent about date and location, so confirm with the organizers or the Montrose visitor office before you go.

Colorado Mountain Winefest

The Colorado Mountain Winefest has celebrated Colorado wine in Palisade for 35 years. For 2026 it was reimagined as a single-day, capacity-limited food and wine tasting at Riverbend Park on Saturday, September 19. Most of Colorado's wineries are in the Grand Valley American Viticultural Area around Palisade, with a second cluster in the West Elks AVA near Paonia, one of the highest-elevation wine regions in North America. Check coloradowinefest.com for the current format.

Apples, chile and the end of the season

As September turns to October, the Western Slope moves on to apples and pears, and the Front Range farmers' markets fill with roasting drums of green chile. Pueblo chile, grown on the Arkansas River east of Pueblo, is the variety Coloradans argue about, and it is in season at the end of summer.

Every week, not once a year

Farmers' Markets

If your trip does not line up with a festival, a farmers' market is the simplest way to eat the Colorado season. Most run on weekends from late spring into fall. The Boulder County Farmers Market, the long-running market downtown on 13th Street, is the best-known on the Front Range, and nearly every mountain town runs a weekly summer market of its own. Opening dates and hours change annually, so check the market's own site for the current season.

Planning

Tips For Food And Drink Events

  • Book Aspen and Palisade weekends early. Both are small towns with limited lodging; Grand Junction has far more rooms than Palisade.
  • Plan your ride home. Colorado enforces impaired-driving laws strictly, and mountain roads are unforgiving. Use festival shuttles, transit or a designated driver.
  • Buy fruit for the trip home carefully. Peaches bruise in a hot car; bring a cooler.
  • Expect weather. Late-summer festivals on the Western Slope can be very hot at midday and stormy in the afternoon.

Common questions

Food And Drink Festival Questions

Where is the Great American Beer Festival held?

The festival is held in Denver. For 2026 it moved from the Colorado Convention Center to Levitt Pavilion in south Denver, its first outdoor edition, with sessions on October 10 and 11.

When are Palisade peaches in season?

Palisade peaches are generally harvested from midsummer into September, depending on the variety and the year. The Palisade Peach Festival in late August marks the peak of the season.

Does Colorado have wine country?

Yes. Most Colorado wineries are in the Grand Valley around Palisade and Grand Junction, and a smaller cluster is in the West Elks area near Paonia and Hotchkiss. The Colorado Mountain Winefest in Palisade each September is the best-known celebration of Colorado wine.
